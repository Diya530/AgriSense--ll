# Pest & Disease Management Basics

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

IPM combines multiple approaches — resistant varieties, crop rotation,
biological control (natural predators, pheromone traps), cultural practices
(field sanitation, proper spacing), and chemical control only when needed and
at the right threshold — rather than relying on preventive chemical spraying
alone. This approach is generally recommended both for cost savings and to
slow the development of pesticide resistance.

## Common Warning Signs

- Yellowing that starts from older (lower) leaves often points toward a
  nitrogen or general nutrient deficiency rather than disease, especially if
  spread gradually across the field.
- Yellowing/spots concentrated on younger leaves, or appearing in patches
  rather than uniformly, more often suggests a pathogen, pest, or localized
  water-stress/waterlogging issue.
- Sudden wilting of an otherwise healthy-looking plant can indicate root
  disease, vascular wilt, or root damage (pests or waterlogging) rather than
  simple water stress.
- Sticky residue on leaves with ants present often indicates sap-sucking
  insects (aphids, mealybugs) producing honeydew.

## Fungal Disease Risk Factors

Fungal diseases (blights, rusts, mildews) generally thrive in humid, cool to
moderate temperature, overcast conditions with poor air circulation. Dense
planting and excess nitrogen (which promotes lush, susceptible growth) can
both increase risk. Crop rotation and resistant varieties are usually the
most cost-effective long-term prevention.

## When Diagnosis Is Uncertain

Visual diagnosis from a photo alone (whether by a person or an AI system) is
inherently limited — many plant issues share overlapping visible symptoms
(e.g., certain nutrient deficiencies can look similar to early-stage
disease). Confirming with a local agricultural extension officer, plant
clinic, or a lab test (for serious/valuable crops) is recommended before
committing to significant chemical treatment, especially when confidence in
the diagnosis is low.
