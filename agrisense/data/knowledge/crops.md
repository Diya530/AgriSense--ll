# Crop Cultivation Basics (India-focused)

This is a starter knowledge base condensed from general agricultural-extension
guidance (ICAR/university-style recommendations). It is intentionally general —
always adjust for local variety, soil test results, and current-season advisory
from your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agricultural extension office.

## Wheat

Wheat is a Rabi (winter) crop, sown roughly October to December depending on
region, and harvested February to April. It prefers cool weather during
vegetative growth and warmer, dry conditions during grain filling. Typical
recommended nitrogen application is split across sowing, first irrigation
(crown root stage), and later growth stages rather than applied all at once,
since split doses reduce nitrogen loss and improve uptake. Ideal soil pH is
roughly neutral (6.0-7.5). Excess irrigation at sowing can cause poor
germination; the crown root initiation stage (about 20-25 days after sowing)
is usually the most critical irrigation stage.

## Rice

Rice is typically a Kharif (monsoon) crop in most of India, transplanted after
raising nursery seedlings, though direct-seeded rice is increasingly used to
save water. It needs standing water for much of its growth cycle in
traditional puddled cultivation, though alternate wetting and drying (AWD)
techniques can reduce water use significantly without much yield loss. Ideal
soil pH is slightly acidic to neutral (5.5-7.0). Nitrogen is usually applied
in 2-3 split doses.

## Cotton

Cotton is a long-duration Kharif crop needing warm temperatures throughout its
growth cycle and is sensitive to waterlogging, especially in early growth.
Balanced potassium application is particularly important for boll development
and fiber quality. Pink bollworm and whitefly are common pest concerns in many
regions; integrated pest management (IPM) and pheromone traps are commonly
recommended over relying solely on chemical sprays.

## Mustard

Mustard is a Rabi oilseed crop, relatively drought-tolerant compared to wheat,
and is often grown with fewer irrigations. It prefers cooler temperatures and
is sensitive to frost at flowering. Aphid infestation is a common concern in
later growth stages and is best monitored regularly rather than sprayed
preventively.

## Potato

Potato prefers cool growing conditions and well-drained, slightly acidic soil
(pH 5.0-6.5). Consistent soil moisture (not waterlogged) is important,
especially during tuber bulking. Late blight is a major fungal disease risk in
humid, cool, overcast conditions and is best managed with timely preventive
measures rather than after visible spread.

## General Notes

- Crop water requirement varies significantly by growth stage — flowering and
  grain/fruit filling stages are usually the most water-sensitive.
- Soil testing every 1-2 seasons is generally recommended over guessing
  fertilizer needs, since nutrient levels change with cropping history.
- Crop rotation (e.g., alternating cereals with legumes) helps manage
  soil-borne pests/diseases and can improve soil nitrogen naturally through
  legume nitrogen fixation.
